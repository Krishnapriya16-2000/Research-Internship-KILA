Haritha Karma Sena (HKS) Performance Analysis – Thrissur District

Project Overview

This project presents a statistical evaluation of the operational efficiency, socioeconomic challenges, and public perception of the Haritha Karma Sena (HKS) waste management initiative across 11 Grama Panchayats in Thrissur, Kerala.

The study analyzed waste collection infrastructure, citizen compliance, occupational health risks, and digital adoption of the Haritha Mithram application among HKS workers.

Objectives

Assess the effectiveness of decentralized waste management systems.
Evaluate public satisfaction and waste disposal compliance.
Analyze occupational health and workplace challenges faced by HKS workers.
Study adoption and usability of the Haritha Mithram mobile application.
Provide data-driven recommendations for improving operational efficiency.

Study Area & Sample Size

Location: 11 Grama Panchayats in Thrissur District, Kerala
Citizen Survey: 562 respondents
HKS Worker Survey: 171 Green Technicians

Research Design & Framework

Sampling Technique: Multi-stage stratified random sampling across 11 Grama Panchayats in Thrissur District.
Citizen Survey: 562 respondents
HKS Workforce Survey: 171 active Green Technicians

Tools Used: Structured questionnaires analyzed using R Programming Language.

Statistical Methods Used
Pearson’s Chi-Square Test 
    Used to determine relationships between categorical variables and evaluate statistical independence.

Fisher’s Exact Test
    Applied when contingency table cells contained low expected frequencies ($E_{ij} < 5$), ensuring reliable significance testing.

Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K-S) Test
    Used as a non-parametric method to verify data normality and distribution patterns.

Mann–Whitney U Test
    Implemented to compare ordinal and ranked responses between independent respondent groups.

Key Findings:

Public Perception & Waste Collection:

95.9% of citizens confirmed reliable monthly doorstep waste collection.
79.3% reported being satisfied or very satisfied with HKS services.

Despite high satisfaction:

74.3% of HKS workers experienced public reluctance.
76.0% reported hostile interactions during collection activities.

Hostility was primarily reported from:

82.44% residential households
17.56% commercial establishments

Occupational Health Analysis:

75.4% of workers were above 40 years of age.
56.1% worked 25–30 days per month.
66.7% worked fixed 8-hour shifts.
58.5% developed health problems after joining HKS.

Major Health Issues:

Skin allergies/infections – 34.3%
Musculoskeletal & back pain – 15.7%
Respiratory problems – 13.7%

Compliance & Digital Adoption:

34.9% of citizens were unaware of state waste disposal penalties.
36.5% lacked awareness of the Haritha Keralam Mission objectives.
24.0% reported illegal roadside waste dumping still occurring.
69.0% of workers actively used the Haritha Mithram application.
31.0% faced technical or training-related barriers while using the app.

Recommendations: 

Ergonomic & Safety Improvements:
Provide heavy-duty PPE kits and puncture-resistant gloves.
Introduce lightweight wheeled waste collection carts to reduce physical strain.
Community Awareness Programs

Conduct ward-level campaigns on:
Waste segregation rules
Legal penalties
Waste collection schedules
Respectful interaction with HKS workers
Digital Optimization

Improve Haritha Mithram app usability through:
Offline synchronization support
Simplified UI/UX
Icon-based navigation for older workers

Outcome

The analysis identified major gaps in public awareness, occupational safety, and digital accessibility while also highlighting the strong operational performance of the HKS waste collection system. The findings can support local governments in improving sustainable waste management policies, worker welfare initiatives, and citizen engagement strategies.
